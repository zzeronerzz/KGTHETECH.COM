RF Hardware · Standard

Cantenna — Standard

$50

Directional 2.4GHz Wi-Fi antenna. 14dBi gain, 22° beam, up to 300m range. Accurately built to 1/4 wavelength spec. Female N-type connector. No tripod included.

This is a 2.4 GHz Cantenna designed for Wi-Fi range extension. It's especially useful for those seeking much needed longer range, and it's ideal for pentesters (penetration testing, ethical hackers, white hat hackers). Or anyone else looking for range out of their Wi-Fi adapter.

It works very well, providing great range and excellent penetration through walls for a reliable connection. Built personally to ensure a professional finish.

Connection Type: Female N-type connector. You will need a Male N-type to SMA connector for your Wi-Fi card. This cantenna is accurately built, adhering to the 1/4 wavelength guide for the can's diameter. This ensures precise functionality — it's not just a can with a random metal pole placed inside.

This 2.4GHz Cantenna is designed to connect to signals up to 300 meters away. It has a 14dBi gain that concentrates the signal into a narrow 22-degree beam, making it highly directional and requiring precise aiming at the access point. Despite the narrow beam, it performs well through obstacles like trees, making it effective even without a direct line of sight.

Package Includes:

  • Custom built 2.4GHz Cantenna BLACK (as shown in pictures)
  • No tripod included — NOTE: Cantenna takes a 1/4th thread for mounting, NOT 3/8ths

Security Hardware

ESP32 Wardriver Kit

$150

Custom-assembled ESP32 wardriving platform. Dual Wi-Fi antennas, GPS, NRF24, MicroSD on one board. C++ firmware writes Wigle.net-compatible records to SD. No phone, no cloud, fully standalone.

This ESP32 Wi-Fi Signal Survey & Developer Kit is an open-hardware electronics kit for hobbyists, educators, and RF/IoT developers. It's ideal for learning about wireless signals, prototype development, and lab-based signal-strength experiments.

The kit contains an ESP32-based board with antenna connectors, a MicroSD socket for user-controlled logging, and a GPS antenna connector for timestamp/location experiments you have legal permission to run. Ships as a development kit with minimal demonstration firmware to help you get started.

The firmware handles simultaneous Wi-Fi scanning and GPS fix acquisition, writing timestamped, GPS-tagged signal records directly to the SD card in Wigle.net-compatible format. No phone required, no cloud dependency — the device operates fully standalone in the field.

Every unit is hand-assembled and tested. Antenna placements, connector types, and firmware configuration chosen for real-world signal survey work — not a hobbyist breadboard prototype.

What's Included:

  • ESP32 development board (assembled) — minimal demo firmware
  • 1x GPS antenna connector (antenna included)
  • 2x Wi-Fi antenna connectors (antennas included)
  • 1x MicroSD socket (card included; formatted for user data)
  • Wiring harness & quickstart sheet

Legal Notice: Intended for authorized signal testing, academic lab use, and electronics hobby projects only. Do not use to scan, log, map, or access networks you do not own or have explicit permission to test. Buyer solely responsible for compliance with all applicable laws.

THE STORY

Self-Taught Since 2004

KGTHETECH is a 40-year-old self-taught cybersecurity specialist and hardware maker from New York, now based in Aurora, Colorado. Working with networks and systems since 2004 — back when the proper terms weren't even known. TCP/IP learned 20+ years ago by clicking around, breaking things, fixing them, and figuring it out.

KG The Tech was founded as a one-man operation focused on IT services, cybersecurity tools, graphic design, and commercial photography. Over the years: PC Tech at Insight Global/Deutsche Bank, stints at Home Depot and Top Shelf Electric, investor relations at DF King/Fidelity. Attended City Tech CUNY studying architecture, graduated from High School of Art & Design in New York.

Life Has Been a Roller Coaster

The last 34 years have been rough. Homelessness, financial struggles, anger, and gaps in life that most people can't imagine. Been broke, frustrated, and at times completely defeated.

In 2022, a car accident caused a severe back injury. In 2023, the injury was re-aggravated in Oklahoma. Then, while working at King Soopers in Colorado, another back injury caused temporary paralysis — with zero compensation. Chronic back pain is a daily reality to this day.

To make income, plasma donations fill the gap — a process that takes a physical toll but provides just enough to survive. The Amazon Vine program helped source parts for builds, though that's no longer active. Parts now come from AliExpress and wherever deals can be found.

Building for Mental Health

Building cybersecurity tools, ethical hacking devices, cyberdecks, antennas, and electronics isn't just a hobby or a side hustle — it's therapy. It's proof that even when the world is crashing down, something functional, valuable, and real can still be created.

Cantennas built from soup cans and car exhaust pipes. ESP32 signal survey kits. Cyberdecks running Raspberry Pi, Kali Linux, SDR-RTL, and wireless adapters. Even a complete FPV drone — though the goggles to fly it still aren't affordable yet.

All of it documented on TikTok at @KGTheTech — raw process, no hand-holding, no fancy editing. Just the journey.

From New York, Grew Up in Poverty

From New York. Grew up in poverty. Black. Representing hip-hop culture and the streets. This isn't a curated brand identity — this is real. "Hacking on a Budget" is literal: no 3D printer, so materials get made from ABS plastic by hand. No professional photography budget, but trained as a commercial photographer — so it gets done.

Building from nothing, creating something, and making it work — no matter what.

The Goal: Professional Cybersecurity

Working toward CompTIA Network+, Security+, and CySA+ certifications. Active on TryHackMe, constantly practicing. A portfolio of real-world skills built through doing — signal analysis, RF development, network security, and hardware hacking.

Every dollar from product sales, subscriptions, and donations goes directly toward: parts, certifications, equipment, and survival.
